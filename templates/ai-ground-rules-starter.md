# AI Ground Rules — one-page starter

A short, honest set of rules a small business can adopt this week. It's deliberately light: enough to make AI use safe and consistent, without a 20-page policy nobody reads.

This is the **free starter**. It is *not* a substitute for a full AI use policy with a data-classification scheme, an audit trail, and staff certification — that's the paid [AI Literacy Compliance Pack](https://aitutorium.com), which exists because regulated and client-serving businesses need to *prove* their people were trained (EU AI Act Article 4). Use this to start; upgrade when you need evidence, not just intent.

Copy the block below, fill the brackets, and share it with your team.

---

**[Business name] — AI Ground Rules**

*Adopted: [date] · Owner: [name/role] · Review: [date + 12 months]*

**1. What we use AI for.**
Drafting and speeding up everyday work — proposals, emails, job ads, meeting notes, first-draft documents, research, and content. AI gives us a first draft or a faster start; a person always finishes and checks it.

**2. What never goes into an AI tool.**
No client confidential information, personal data (names + details), contracts, financial or payroll records, or anything we wouldn't be comfortable seeing on a public website. If in doubt, leave it out or ask [name].

**3. A human checks everything before it leaves the building.**
No AI-drafted content reaches a client, a customer, or the public without a person reading every word. AI can be confidently wrong — names, numbers, and facts are our job to verify.

**4. We don't let AI decide about people.**
AI does not make or materially shape decisions about hiring, performance, or anything affecting a person's livelihood. It can help draft a job ad or summarise a brief; the decision stays with a named human.

**5. Approved tools.**
The tools we use for work are: [list your 1–3, e.g. an enterprise ChatGPT/Copilot account]. Free personal accounts are fine for non-sensitive, non-client work only. To add a tool, ask [name].

**6. Ask, don't guess.**
If you're unsure whether something is okay, ask [name/channel] before doing it. Asking is always the right call. We'd rather answer a question than clean up an incident.

---

> **Why keep it this short?** A one-page rule people actually read beats a policy that sits in a drawer. When you need to *demonstrate* compliance — to a client, an insurer, or a regulator — that's when you move up to a full policy, assessment, and certified training. Until then, these six rules cover the great majority of day-to-day decisions.
