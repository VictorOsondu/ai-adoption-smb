# AI Ground Rules — one-page starter

A short, honest set of rules a small business can adopt this week. It's deliberately light: enough to make AI use safe and consistent, without a 20-page policy nobody reads.

This is the **free starter**. It is *not* a substitute for a full AI use policy with a data-classification scheme, staff training records, and an audit trail. Use this to start; add stronger evidence when clients, insurers, regulators, sector rules, or AI literacy obligations require it.

Last reviewed: 2026-07-24. See [sources and verification notes](../sources.md).

Copy the block below, fill the brackets, and share it with your team.

---

**[Business name] — AI Ground Rules**

*Adopted: [date] · Owner: [name/role] · Review: [date + 12 months]*

**1. What we use AI for.**
Drafting and speeding up everyday work — proposals, emails, job ads, meeting notes, first-draft documents, research, and content. AI gives us a first draft or a faster start; a person always finishes and checks it.

**2. What never goes into a public AI tool.**
No client confidential information, customer personal data, employee records, contracts, financial or payroll records, credentials, private links, proprietary code, health or safeguarding information, regulated data, or anything we wouldn't be comfortable seeing on a public website. If in doubt, leave it out or ask [name].

**3. A human checks everything before it leaves the building.**
No AI-drafted content reaches a client, a customer, or the public without a person reading every word. AI can be confidently wrong — names, numbers, claims, citations, prices, and facts are our job to verify.

**4. We don't let AI decide about people.**
AI does not make or materially shape decisions about hiring, performance, or anything affecting a person's livelihood. It can help draft a job ad or summarise a brief; the decision stays with a named human.

**5. Approved tools.**
The tools we use for work are: [list your 1–3, e.g. a business/enterprise AI account]. Free personal accounts are fine for public or non-sensitive, non-client work only. Approved work tools have owners, allowed use cases, data limits, and review dates. To add a tool, ask [name].

**6. No unauthorised commitments.**
AI must not set prices, refunds, discounts, policy exceptions, eligibility decisions, legal or financial advice, or promises to customers or clients unless a named human with authority approves it.

**7. Report mistakes quickly.**
If confidential data, personal data, credentials, or a bad customer-facing output goes into or comes out of AI, report it through [channel] so it can be handled.

**8. Ask, don't guess.**
If you're unsure whether something is okay, ask [name/channel] before doing it. Asking is always the right call. We'd rather answer a question than clean up an incident.

---

> **Why keep it this short?** A one-page rule people actually read beats a policy that sits in a drawer. When you need to demonstrate a stronger process to a client, insurer, regulator, or sector body, move up to a full policy, assessment, training record, and audit trail. Until then, these eight rules cover the great majority of day-to-day decisions.
